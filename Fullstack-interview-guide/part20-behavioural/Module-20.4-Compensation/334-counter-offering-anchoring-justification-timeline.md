> Part 20 — Behavioural & Leadership · Compensation & Negotiation
> Full Stack Interview Guide · Hruday D · 2026

---

## ⚡ 60-Second Revision Card
> Read this the night before an interview. Nothing else needed.

- **A counter-offer is expected**: companies always leave room; the first offer is not their best offer for 80%+ of candidates who negotiate; if you don't counter, you leave money on the table by design
- **The anchor principle**: whoever names a number first sets the anchor; when you counter, name your specific number first ("I was targeting ₹40L fixed") rather than asking "is there flexibility?" — vague asks get "this is our best" responses; specific asks get specific responses
- **Justification is not begging**: saying "I'd like more" is a plea; saying "Naukri 2024-25 data shows ₹38-44L is the median for this role — I'm asking for ₹40L, which is in range" is a fact-based negotiation; justify with market data, not personal need
- **The one-time only rule**: counter once, clearly, substantively; if you counter three times with incrementally small asks, you appear uncertain and create recruiter fatigue; one well-prepared counter is more effective than three uncertain ones
- **Timeline pressure (real and artificial)**: "We need an answer by Friday" — in 90% of cases, this is soft; a polite "I'm very close to a decision and I just need 1 more day to confirm the equity component" almost always works; only accept if it's for a genuine HR deadline (eg. offer paperwork cut-off)
- **The "this is our best offer" response**: don't accept it at face value; respond with "I understand — can we at least see if there's flexibility on joining bonus or equity grant to close the gap?" — pivoting to a different component often finds room when the base is locked

## 1. One-Line Definition
A counter-offer is a specific, market-anchored, single response to an initial offer that states your target number with justification, asks clearly and without apology, and positions the decision back in the company's hands without applying pressure.

---

## 2. The Problem It Solves

Two engineers get the same offer of ₹35L from Razorpay. Both have the same market rate justification (₹40L based on Naukri data). Engineer A asks "is there any flexibility?" and is told "this is our standard band for this role." Engineer B says "I was targeting ₹40L based on market data for senior roles in Bangalore — is there flexibility to close toward ₹40L?" and is offered ₹38L.

Same starting offer. Same market data. Engineer B got ₹3L/year more (₹12L over 4 years factoring probable increments) because they named a specific number with a justification.

The difference is not confidence or personality. The difference is structure: specific number + market justification + direct ask.

## 3. How It Works Internally

### The Counter-Offer Formula

```
Step 1: OPEN WITH POSITIVE INTENT
  "I'm really excited about this role and the team — 
   I want this to work."
  
  Why: sets a collaborative tone; you're not an adversary;
  you are both trying to find a mutually agreeable number.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 2: ACKNOWLEDGE WHAT YOU LIKE
  "The equity component and the role scope are exactly 
   what I was hoping for."
  
  Why: shows you've reviewed the offer carefully; signals 
  you're not being greedy across the board; gives them 
  something to feel good about.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 3: NAME THE GAP + YOUR NUMBER
  "The fixed base is a bit lower than my target. Based on 
   Naukri salary data for Senior Full Stack roles in Bangalore, 
   the median range is ₹38–44L. I was targeting ₹40L fixed. 
   Is there any room to close toward that?"
  
  Why: specific number (not a range — ranges get the floor);
  market data (not personal need); direct ask.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 4: SILENCE
  After your ask, stop talking. Wait for the response.
  
  Most candidates fill the silence with concessions:
  "...but I'm flexible, I understand if it's not possible, 
  whatever works for you..."
  
  The silence is not adversarial. The recruiter is checking 
  their room. Let them.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 5: RESPOND TO THEIR RESPONSE
  Response A: "The best we can do is ₹37L."
  → "₹37L — can we also look at a joining bonus to bridge 
     the gap? A one-time ₹1L joining bonus would close it 
     for me." (Pivot to another component)
  
  Response B: "Our band is fixed — ₹35L is our max."
  → "I understand. Could we revisit after 6 months? If I 
     can agree to an explicit performance review at 6 months 
     with a target above ₹37L, I'd be comfortable starting 
     at ₹35L." (Add a future commitment)
  
  Response C: "We can do ₹40L."
  → "That works. Thank you." (Stop. Don't push further.)
```

### Components to Negotiate Beyond Base

```
If base is locked, pivot to:

1. Joining Bonus
   "Is there a joining bonus we can add to close the gap?"
   Often easier to grant because it's one-time, not recurring headcount cost.

2. Equity / ESOP Grant
   "Can we increase the ESOP grant size?"
   At growth-stage companies, equity is often more flexible than base.

3. Early Performance Review
   "Can we add a 6-month performance review with a 
    defined salary target?"
   This adds a contractual milestone rather than a one-time payment.

4. Remote Work / WFH Policy
   "Is there flexibility on the WFH policy?"
   Not monetary, but affects commute cost and quality of life.

5. Role Title / Level
   "Is there flexibility on the level grade?"
   Level up = higher future increment percentages and faster promotion pace.
```

## 4. The Script

### Wrong Way — Vague Counter

```
Recruiter: "The offer is ₹35L CTC."

❌ Weak counter:
"That's a bit lower than what I was hoping for. 
I've had some other discussions and they were offering 
a bit more. Is there any way you could improve it? 
I'm really interested but I need it to make sense 
financially."

Problems:
  - "A bit lower" — how much lower? What's your ask?
  - "Other discussions offering more" — vague; sounds like bluffing
  - "A bit more" — not a number; recruiter has nothing to engage with
  - "Make sense financially" — personal need, not market data
  - No specific ask = no specific answer
```

```
✅ Structured counter:

"I'm genuinely excited about the role — the technical scope 
and the team are exactly what I was looking for.

I've had a chance to review the offer fully. The equity 
structure and the joining bonus are really good. On the 
fixed base, I was targeting ₹40L. I've done the research — 
Naukri and AmbitionBox both show ₹38–44L as the median for 
Senior Full Stack roles in Bangalore with 7+ years. My ask 
of ₹40L is in the middle of that range.

Is there flexibility to close toward ₹40L on the fixed base?"

[Silence — wait for response]

If response is: "We can do ₹37L — that's our maximum for this band."

"Thank you — I appreciate you checking. ₹37L plus the equity 
does close the gap somewhat. Could we also add ₹1.5L as a 
joining bonus to bridge the remaining difference? That would 
let me say yes today."

Why this works:
✅ Specific number (₹40L)
✅ Market data anchor (Naukri/AmbitionBox)
✅ Positive framing (excited about the role)
✅ Pivot when base is locked (joining bonus)
✅ Closes with a clear path to yes ("would let me say yes today")
```

## 5. Interview Questions & Model Answers

### Q1 — Live Scenario
**Recruiter says:** "We've extended our best offer. There's no room to negotiate."

**Hruday's response:**
> "I appreciate you checking. I understand if the base band is fixed. Can we look at any flexibility in the joining bonus? A one-time ₹1L joining bonus would close the gap for me and let me confirm today."
>
> *Why*: The recruiter said "base band is fixed" — not "the entire package is fixed." Joining bonuses come from a different budget than salary. Pivoting to a different component gives them a yes path.

---

### Q2 — Timeline Pressure
**Recruiter says:** "We need an answer by end of day today."

**Hruday's response:**
> "I'm very close to a decision — I'm genuinely excited about the role. I just want to make sure I've completed my review of the equity component before I confirm. Could I have until tomorrow morning? I'll have an answer by 10am."
>
> *Why*: One specific additional request (equity review) + a concrete new deadline (10am tomorrow) is almost always granted. It's not a delay tactic — it's a reasonable request that reasonable recruiters grant.

## 6. The Traps

| Trap | What most candidates do | What Hruday does |
|------|------------------------|------------------|
| Range instead of number | "I was hoping for somewhere between ₹38 and ₹42L" | A range gets the floor — always; name the number you want: "₹40L" |
| Apologising | "I know this might be a lot to ask, but..." | Direct ask without apology: "I was targeting ₹40L — is there flexibility there?" |
| Multiple counters | Gets ₹37L, then asks for ₹39L, then asks for ₹38L | Counter once, firmly; if they can't reach your number, pivot to a different component |
| Emotional language | "I really need this to work" | Market language: "Naukri shows ₹38-44L for this role" — data, not emotion |

## 7. Hruday's Real Experience Hook
> "The negotiation that taught me the most was when I asked 'is there any flexibility?' and was told 'no, this is our best offer.' I accepted. A colleague who joined two weeks later in the same role negotiated ₹4L higher — she had asked for a specific number with specific market data. The recruiter's 'best offer' was not the ceiling; it was the floor of negotiation for candidates who came prepared. The lesson: 'this is our best offer' is almost never true. It's what they say to candidates who don't have a number ready."

## 8. Scale Evolution

**First job / junior level →** Simple base negotiation; market data from Glassdoor or Naukri; one counter for ₹1-3L improvement; joining bonus to bridge small gaps.

**Senior / current level →** Full package: base + variable + equity + joining bonus; three data sources (Naukri + AmbitionBox + peer network); component pivots when base is locked.

**FAANG / staff level →** Total compensation over 4-year vesting window; RSU cliff negotiations; level negotiation (SDE2 vs SDE3 is worth ₹20-40L/yr difference at Microsoft/Adobe); use Levels.fyi for equity benchmarking across companies.
