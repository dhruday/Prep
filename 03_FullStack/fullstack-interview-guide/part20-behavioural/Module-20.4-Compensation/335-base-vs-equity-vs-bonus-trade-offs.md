# Base vs Equity vs Bonus Trade-offs
> Part 20 — Behavioural & Leadership · Compensation & Negotiation
> Full Stack Interview Guide · Hruday D · 2026

---

## ⚡ 60-Second Revision Card
> Read this the night before an interview. Nothing else needed.

- **Base is the compounding asset**: every future raise is a percentage of base; a ₹2L higher base at 7 years compounded at 20% per year across 3 jobs adds ₹40L+ to lifetime earnings; equity and joining bonuses don't compound the same way
- **Equity value depends on stage**: pre-IPO ESOP is a lottery ticket (could be 0, could be a windfall); post-IPO RSU at Microsoft or Adobe is close to cash (market-priced, liquid at vest); treat them differently in your calculation
- **Variable/bonus is the least reliable component**: it is paid "on-target" — 100% payout is the exception, not the rule; use 70-80% of stated variable as your expected value
- **The 4-year total comp formula**: `Base × 4 + (Equity grant at current value) + Joining bonus` — compare offers on this number, not CTC alone
- **When to weight equity over base**: company is pre-IPO + has a documented exit path (secondary round at known valuation, IPO filing, acquisition talks); you can tolerate 2-4 years of base sacrifice
- **When to weight base over equity**: company is post-IPO and RSU price is volatile; you need predictable income; the base anchors the next offer you receive (equity from company A doesn't carry over to company B negotiations)

---

## 1. One-Line Definition
The decision between base, equity, and bonus is a risk-adjusted total compensation trade-off: base is predictable and compounds, equity has high upside but uncertain realization, and bonus is deferred at-risk income that should be treated as 70–80% of stated target value.

---

## 2. The Problem It Solves

Two offers on the table:

**Offer A — Startup (pre-IPO):**
- Fixed base: ₹32L
- Variable: ₹6L on-target
- ESOP: ₹24L grant (4-year vest, 1-year cliff)
- Joining bonus: none
- CTC stated: ₹38L (excluding ESOPs)

**Offer B — Product company (post-IPO / stable):**
- Fixed base: ₹38L
- Variable: ₹4L on-target
- RSU: ₹8L (4-year vest, market priced)
- Joining bonus: ₹1L
- CTC stated: ₹42L

Naively, Offer B looks ₹4L better. But:
- If the startup exits at 5× in 3 years, the ESOP at ₹24L grant is worth ₹120L
- If the startup doesn't exit, the ESOP is worth zero
- Offer B's base is ₹6L higher — that ₹6L compounds in future negotiations

The right choice depends on your risk tolerance, cash requirements, and your view of the startup's exit probability — not on which headline CTC number is larger.

## 3. How It Works Internally

### Component-by-Component Analysis

```
1. FIXED BASE
   What it is: guaranteed monthly salary × 12
   
   Why it matters most for career compounding:
   - Future offer calculations start from current CTC
   - Annual increments are a % of base (typically 15-30%)
   - Base determines PF contribution (Employer PF = 12% of basic)
   - Higher base = higher gratuity eligibility after 5 years
   
   Risk: very low (guaranteed as long as employed)
   Upside: capped by annual increment policy
   
   Strategy: Maximise this unless the equity trade-off is genuinely 
   compelling (pre-IPO with real exit path).

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

2. VARIABLE / PERFORMANCE BONUS
   What it is: annual bonus paid on achieving performance targets
   
   Types:
   - Individual target (MBO): based on your KPIs (controllable)
   - Company Performance: based on org revenue/profit (less controllable)
   - Spot bonus: ad hoc for specific achievements (unpredictable)
   
   The 70-80% rule:
   Most companies pay 80-90% of target in good years, 
   40-60% in flat years, 0% when under pressure.
   Use 75% of target variable as your expected annual value.
   
   Questions to ask:
   "What was the average payout as a % of target in the last 3 years?"
   "Is the variable tied to individual, team, or company performance?"
   
   Risk: medium (partly within your control)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

3. EQUITY

   Pre-IPO ESOP (startup / growth-stage):
   - Granted at a strike price (often low at early stage)
   - Vests over 4 years with 1-year cliff
   - Value realised only at exit (IPO or acquisition)
   - Could be worth 0 if company fails or exit is below valuation
   
   Calculate expected value:
   "₹24L grant at current valuation × P(exit) × expected exit multiple"
   Eg: ₹24L × 40% chance of exit × 3× multiple = ₹28.8L expected value
   This is still a rough estimate — but it forces you to think in probability.
   
   Post-IPO RSU (Microsoft / Adobe / Swiggy post-IPO):
   - Market-priced at grant date; value moves with stock
   - Vests quarterly or annually
   - Close to cash equivalent after vest (liquid with trading restrictions)
   - At Microsoft/Adobe, RSUs are a meaningful % of total comp
   
   Use Levels.fyi for current RSU data at target companies.
   
   Risk: high (pre-IPO) → medium (post-IPO liquid large-cap)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

4. JOINING BONUS (SIGN-ON)
   What it is: one-time payment on joining, sometimes split over year 1
   
   It is often used to compensate you for:
   - Unvested equity you're leaving behind at your current employer
   - Notice period buyout (if current employer deducts)
   - Bridging a salary gap when base negotiation hits a ceiling
   
   Clawback clause:
   Many signing bonuses require repayment if you leave before 12-18 months.
   Always read the clawback terms before factoring in the joining bonus.
   
   Strategy: treat joining bonus as one-time income, not recurring CTC.
   Don't inflate your next offer expectation based on joining bonus alone.
   
   Risk: repayment risk if you leave within clawback window
```

### 4-Year Total Comp (4YTC) Calculation

```
OFFER A (Startup):
  Base: ₹32L × 4 = ₹128L
  Variable (75% of ₹6L): ₹4.5L × 4 = ₹18L
  ESOP (expected value): ₹24L × 40% × 3× = ₹28.8L
  Joining bonus: 0
  4YTC = ₹174.8L

OFFER B (Product company):
  Base: ₹38L × 4 = ₹152L
  Variable (75% of ₹4L): ₹3L × 4 = ₹12L
  RSU (market valued): ₹8L (liquid, assumed flat growth) = ₹8L
  Joining bonus: ₹1L
  4YTC = ₹173L

In this case: nearly equivalent on 4YTC. Offer B has lower risk 
(guaranteed base is ₹24L more over 4 years). Offer A has higher 
upside if the ESOP plays out at 3× or more.

Decision: Offer B if you need predictability. Offer A if you 
believe in the startup exit story and can absorb the base gap.
```

## 4. Decision Framework

### Wrong Way — Comparing Headline CTC

```
❌ "Offer B says ₹42L and Offer A says ₹38L. 
    Offer B is better."

Problems:
  - CTC often includes employer PF contribution, gratuity accrual, 
    food coupons, insurance premium — items with no take-home value
  - Equity is sometimes included in CTC at grant value, 
    not vested value
  - Variable is shown at 100% target, not expected payout

Never compare CTCs. Compare fixed base / in-hand base 
and 4-year total comp separately.
```

```
✅ Right Framework:

Step 1: Extract each offer into components
  - Fixed monthly gross (what lands in bank)
  - Variable (% of take-home and on what basis)
  - Equity (type, vest schedule, strike price vs current valuation)
  - Joining bonus (any clawback clause?)

Step 2: Calculate 4YTC for each offer

Step 3: Assess risk profile
  - What is your current monthly expense requirement? 
    (Sets the floor for base)
  - Can you absorb a ₹5-8L annual base difference for 
    2-4 years in exchange for equity upside?
  - Do you trust the startup's exit probability? 
    Have you researched their funding stage, investors, 
    and runway?

Step 4: Negotiate the highest leverage component
  - If base is at the band ceiling, push on equity grant size
  - If equity grant is locked, push on joining bonus or 
    performance review timeline
  - Know which component is most flexible before the call
```

## 5. Interview Questions & Model Answers

### Q1 — Offer evaluation approach
**Interviewer:** "How are you comparing the offers you're evaluating?"

**Hruday:**
> "I compare on three dimensions. First, fixed base — because it compounds in future negotiations and is the only guaranteed element. Second, 4-year total comp, which includes equity at expected value (not grant value) and variable at a conservative 75% of target. Third, risk profile — pre-IPO equity needs a probability-weighted view of exit scenarios. I don't compare CTCs because the component mix inside it varies too much between companies to be a useful comparison."

---

### Q2 — What trade-off you'd make
**Interviewer:** "Would you take lower base for higher equity at a pre-IPO company?"

**Hruday:**
> "It depends on the exit story and the base gap. If the company has a real exit path — Series C with named investors, secondary market liquidity, or an IPO filing — and the base shortfall is less than ₹6-8L, I'd consider it. I'd do the maths: ₹6L base shortfall over 4 years is ₹24L guaranteed income given up. The ESOP needs to have a plausible expected value above ₹24L to make the trade financially rational. Belief in the company is necessary but not sufficient — the numbers need to work."

## 6. India-Specific CTC Structure Guide

```
TYPICAL CTC STRUCTURE IN INDIA

Basic Salary: ~40-50% of gross CTC
HRA: ~20% of basic salary (tax-exempt if renting)
Special Allowance: remainder of gross (fully taxable)

Employer PF: 12% of basic salary (added to CTC, goes to PF)
Gratuity: 4.81% of basic salary (paid after 5 years of service)

In-hand monthly = (Basic + HRA + Special) - Employee PF (12%) - Tax

---

Ask this question for every offer:
"Can you share the in-hand monthly salary after PF and tax 
deduction at current slabs?"

This is the number that actually goes into your bank account.
CTC minus 20-25% is a rough estimate of in-hand for mid-range 
income brackets.
```

## 7. Hruday's Real Experience Hook
> "When I got my current offer at SAP Labs, I asked for the in-hand monthly breakdown before comparing it to competing offers. Two of the three competing companies were quoting CTCs that included a large employer PF contribution and meal vouchers in the CTC figure — when stripped down to actual take-home, the 'higher' CTC offers were lower monthly income than SAP. The lesson: always ask for the component breakdown, and always compare in-hand salary, not CTC."

## 8. Scale Evolution

**Junior (3-5 yrs) →** Base dominates; equity is often unvalued at junior levels because liquidity events feel distant; focus on maximising base for compounding.

**Senior / current level →** Full 4YTC analysis matters; ESOP/RSU is meaningful at senior grant sizes; variable bonus is a significant % of target; negotiate all three components.

**Staff / Principal →** Equity grant refresh cycles matter (annual RSU refreshes at Microsoft/Adobe are worth as much as base); level matters more than any single component because it determines grant sizes, refresh rates, and the invisible ceiling on future internal growth.
