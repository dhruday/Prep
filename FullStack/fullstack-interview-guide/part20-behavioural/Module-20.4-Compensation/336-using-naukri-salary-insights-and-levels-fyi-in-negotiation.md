> Full Stack Interview Guide · Hruday D · 2026

## ⚡ 60-Second Revision Card
> Read this the night before an interview. Nothing else needed.

- **Data turns a plea into a position**: "I'd like more money" is a request; "Naukri shows ₹38-44L for this role in Bangalore and I'm asking for ₹40L" is a fact-based negotiation — the recruiter has to address data, not just say "sorry"
- **Use 2-3 sources, cross-reference**: a single data point can be dismissed; Naukri + AmbitionBox (or LinkedIn Salary) + Levels.fyi (for FAANG-adjacent companies) creates a defensible range that is hard to argue with
- **Filter properly or the data is useless**: on every platform, filter by city (Bangalore), experience band (6-8 years), role type (Senior Full Stack / Senior Software Engineer), and company type (product vs service); unfiltered data from Naukri includes service company salaries that pull the range down by 30-40%
- **Present ranges, ask for the specific number**: "The market shows ₹38-44L — I'm asking for ₹40L" (not "I want to be at the top of the range" which sounds greedy, and not "somewhere in the lower-middle" which undersells you)
- **Levels.fyi is for total comp at large companies**: Microsoft, Adobe, Google, Amazon in India have structured level-based compensation publicly visible on Levels.fyi; use it for RSU grant benchmarking, not just base salary
- **Your data preparation should take 45 minutes**: set up Naukri salary filter + AmbitionBox company search + Levels.fyi ICT location filter before any negotiation call; come in with 3 screenshots saved; knowing the numbers before the call is what separates prepared from unprepared

## 1. One-Line Definition
Salary data platforms give you the external evidence to convert a personal request into a market-grounded negotiation position — the difference between "I want" and "the market shows."

---

## 2. The Problem It Solves

An interviewer says: "This is our best offer at ₹35L. The role is Senior Full Stack."

You have two options:

**Option A (no data):**
"I was hoping for a bit more. I've heard the market rate is higher."
Recruiter response: "We benchmarked this internally and we're confident we're competitive."
— You have no counter. The recruiter's internal data beats your gut feel every time.

**Option B (with data):**
"I did some research before this call. Naukri's latest data for Senior Full Stack in Bangalore, product companies, 6-8 years experience shows a median range of ₹38-44L. AmbitionBox shows similar numbers for your peer companies. My ask of ₹40L is in the middle of that range. I'm not asking to be an outlier — I'm asking to be at market."
Recruiter response: "Let me check with the hiring manager on the band."

Option B didn't guarantee a raise. But it opened a conversation that Option A closed.

## 3. The Platforms

### Platform 1 — Naukri Salary Insights

```
URL: naukri.com/salary/insights
Primary use: India-specific salary ranges by role + experience + city

HOW TO FILTER CORRECTLY:

1. Role: "Senior Software Engineer" or "Full Stack Developer"
   Do NOT use generic "Software Engineer" — it pulls junior salaries
   
2. Experience: Set to your exact range (eg. 6-8 years)
   
3. Location: Bangalore (not "All India")
   Bangalore product company salaries are 20-35% above 
   the national average; filtering to Bangalore is critical
   
4. Company type: Naukri doesn't have a direct product/service filter,
   but you can cross-compare company names that appear in the 
   data to gauge the sample composition
   
WHAT YOU GET:
- Median salary (best number to anchor to)
- P25-P75 range (use this as your "market range" quote)
- Top companies paying this role (good for comp context)

WHAT TO DO WITH IT:
Screenshot the P25-P75 range and median before the negotiation call.
Cite: "Naukri salary data for Senior Full Stack in Bangalore, 
      updated Q1 2025, shows a median of ₹X with P75 at ₹Y."
```

### Platform 2 — AmbitionBox

```
URL: ambitionbox.com
Primary use: Company-specific salary data (more targeted than Naukri)

HOW TO USE:

1. Search the specific company you're negotiating with
   Eg: "Razorpay Senior Software Engineer Bangalore"
   
2. Filter by experience range and role
   
3. Read the verified salaries (marked with checkmark) 
   — self-reported salaries are a strong signal
   
4. Compare with 2-3 peer companies at the same stage/size
   Eg: Razorpay vs CRED vs PhonePe for fintech-stage comparisons

WHAT YOU GET:
- Company-specific ranges (much more targeted than Naukri)
- Median and distribution for that exact employer
- Interview experience data (useful for prep; salary data useful for negotiation)

HOW TO CITE IT:
"AmbitionBox shows ₹38-40L as the median for Senior Engineers 
at Razorpay — my ask of ₹40L is consistent with that."
```

### Platform 3 — Levels.fyi

```
URL: levels.fyi
Primary use: Total compensation at large tech companies — 
             most reliable for RSU/equity component benchmarking

WHEN TO USE:
- Target company is Adobe, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Flipkart, 
  Atlassian, or any company with a published FAANG-adjacent band
- You need equity/RSU benchmarks, not just base salary
- You're negotiating for a level (eg. SDE2 vs SDE3)

HOW TO FILTER:
1. Country: India (Bangalore is the primary India tier-1 market)
2. Company: select the specific company
3. Level: SDE2, SDE3, L5 (Google), 60 (Microsoft) — 
   company levels are listed with guidance
4. Role: Software Engineer or Full Stack Engineer

WHAT YOU GET:
- Base salary by level (US and India separate)
- RSU grant value by level
- Bonus target by level
- Total comp median by level

CRITICAL INSIGHT: 
At Microsoft India or Adobe India, the difference between 
SDE2 → SDE3 is often ₹15-25L total comp.
Negotiating your level is more valuable than negotiating 
within a level's band at these companies.

HOW TO CITE IT:
"Levels.fyi shows SDE3 total comp at Microsoft India at ₹55-70L 
all-in (base + RSU + bonus). I'm benchmarking my ask accordingly."
```

### Platform 4 — LinkedIn Salary (Secondary)

```
URL: linkedin.com/salary
Primary use: Secondary cross-reference; good for broad ranges
Weakness: includes global data if not filtered; India data 
          is thinner than Naukri
Use it as a third data point to triangulate, not as a primary source.
```

## 4. The Research Workflow (45 Minutes Before Any Offer Call)

### Wrong Way — Researching During the Call

```
❌ "Let me just Google it during the call and see what comes up."

Problems:
  - Recruiters notice when you're researching in real-time; 
    it signals unpreparedness
  - Raw search results are noisy; you need filtered data, 
    not top-of-page results
  - You sound uncertain when you say "I read somewhere that..." 
    vs citing a specific platform with specific filters
```

```
✅ The 45-Minute Pre-Call Research Ritual:

STEP 1 (10 mins): Naukri Salary Insights
  - Filter: role exact keywords + 6-8 years + Bangalore
  - Screenshot: median, P25, P75
  - Note: the specific filter settings you used
  
STEP 2 (10 mins): AmbitionBox
  - Search the specific target company + your role
  - Screenshot: median salary + distribution range
  - Compare: 2 peer companies for context
  
STEP 3 (10 mins): Levels.fyi (if FAANG-adjacent target company)
  - Filter: company + level + India
  - Screenshot: total comp table
  - Note: equity grant value at your target level
  
STEP 4 (5 mins): Build your number
  - Take the median across 2-3 sources
  - Add 5-10% for role specifics 
    (your stack matches exactly, will own design decisions, etc.)
  - Write down: "My ask is ₹X. My floor is ₹Y."
  
STEP 5 (10 mins): Prepare two pivots
  - If base is locked: "Can we look at joining bonus to 
    close the ₹X gap?"
  - If joining bonus is locked: "Can we add an equity 
    grant top-up or a 6-month review?"
  
Save your research in a document before the call.
Having it open during the call means you cite data instantly.
```

## 5. How to Cite Data Without Sounding Scripted

```
❌ Robotic: 
"According to Naukri salary insights data that I downloaded 
at 9:43am today filtered by Senior Full Stack Engineer in 
Bangalore sorted by experience 6 to 8 years..."

✅ Natural:
"I did my research before this call. Naukri and AmbitionBox 
both show ₹38-44L as the range for this type of role and 
experience in Bangalore. I'm asking for ₹40L — right in 
the middle of that range."

Key wording:
- "I did my research" — signals preparation; recruiters 
  respect engineers who come prepared
- Named platforms (not "some website") — credibility signal
- "Right in the middle of that range" — not asking to be 
  an outlier; just market rate; harder to say no to
- One sentence of data + one sentence of your ask — concise
```

## 6. Interview Questions & Model Answers

### Q1 — Live negotiation scenario
**Recruiter:** "₹35L is competitive for this role."

**Hruday:**
> "I appreciate that you've benchmarked internally. I also did research before this call — Naukri and AmbitionBox both show ₹38–44L for Senior Full Stack roles in Bangalore at product companies with 6-8 years experience. My ask of ₹40L sits in the middle of that range. Can we explore whether there's flexibility there?"

---

### Q2 — Source credibility challenge
**Recruiter:** "Those platforms aren't always accurate."

**Hruday:**
> "That's fair — they're self-reported and there's noise. I used two platforms and cross-referenced numbers, and they aligned closely. I also spoke to two peers who recently changed jobs in similar roles, and their data reinforced the same range. I'm not using a single number from a single source — I'm using a consistent signal from several. The ₹40L ask isn't an outlier; it's the median."

## 8. Scale Evolution

**Entry level →** Glassdoor and LinkedIn Salary are sufficient; negotiate in ₹2-5L precision; citing one source is acceptable.

**Senior / current level →** Naukri + AmbitionBox + peer network is the minimum; at this level, the difference between prepared and unprepared is ₹3-8L/year; Levels.fyi matters if targeting Microsoft/Adobe.

**Staff / Principal →** Levels.fyi becomes the primary tool; total comp with equity and leveling data is the key input; the peer network is the most valuable source at this level because public data lags for very senior roles.
